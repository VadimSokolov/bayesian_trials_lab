## What it is

FDA's June 2026 draft defines an umbrella trial as "a trial designed to evaluate multiple drugs concurrently for a single disease or condition". [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform] An umbrella fixes the disease and varies the drug, often assigning patients to arms by biomarker. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform]

## Why it matters

The three master protocol kinds differ in what is held fixed. A basket fixes the drug and varies the population, as in tissue-agnostic approvals built on a molecular marker. A platform fixes the disease and the infrastructure and lets the drugs change over time. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform]

## In the book

In practice the terms overlap. I-SPY 2 is a platform with biomarker-defined signatures, and many baskets are platforms for one drug family. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform] The taxonomy was set out by Woodcock and LaVange (2017), and early reviews of how often the three designs are used are cited. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform] The FDA 2022 final guidance on oncology master protocols recommends a common standard-of-care control for multiple drugs in one disease. [sec-platform]

## Pitfalls

The umbrella is not the same as a platform: an umbrella evaluates drugs concurrently, while a platform lets drugs enter and leave in an ongoing manner. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform] The 2026 FDA text is a draft. [sec-platform]

## Check yourself

- Q: What does an umbrella trial hold fixed? A: The disease. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform]
- Q: Which master protocol fixes the drug instead? A: The basket trial. [20/definitions-master-protocol-basket-umbrella-platform]
