## What it is
FDA issues guidance documents that "contain nonbinding recommendations". [sec-regulatory-fda] In its convention "should" means suggested or recommended, but not required. [sec-regulatory-fda] The statute it applies is section 505(d), and the regulation is 21 CFR 314.126. [3/what-the-statute-says] [3/the-regulation-what-adequate-and-well-controlled-means]

## Why it matters
A sponsor may depart from a guidance but must show that the alternative meets the statute and regulations. [sec-regulatory-fda] A departure without prior discussion with the agency is a risk to the program, which is why meetings exist. [sec-regulatory-fda] A guidance first appears as a draft, comments follow (60 days after the Federal Register notice is usual), and then a final, a revision or a withdrawal. [sec-regulatory-fda]

## In the book
- The adaptive design guidance of November 2019 is final. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019]

- The master protocols revised draft is dated June 2026. [3/master-protocols-for-drug-and-biological-product-development-revised-draft-june-2026]
- FDA offers formal meetings and programs for asking whether it agrees. [sec-regulatory-meetings]
- Expedited programs change the speed and intensity of FDA's interaction, not the standard. [3/other-fda-documents-that-recur]

## Pitfalls
A draft is labeled not for implementation, though sponsors often follow it. [sec-regulatory-fda] The review division that examines an application has the last word on what is adequate for that product. [sec-regulatory-reading]

## Check yourself
- Q: Does "should" in an FDA guidance mean required? A: No, it means suggested or recommended. [sec-regulatory-fda]
- Q: Is a faster expedited review a lower bar? A: No. [3/other-fda-documents-that-recur]
