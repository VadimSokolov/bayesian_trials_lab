## What it is

CALGB 49907 compared capecitabine with standard adjuvant chemotherapy in women aged 65 or older with early-stage breast cancer [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice]. The design planned up to 1,800 patients, with a Bayesian monitoring rule on the noninferiority comparison and noninformative priors [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice].

## Why it matters

It shows the value of futility stopping where the new drug is worse, not just no better [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice]. The criterion was a probability statement with a pre-specified boundary, crossed by the data and not chosen after the fact [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice].

## In the book

At the first interim, after 600 patients, the posterior probability that capecitabine would fail the noninferiority criterion was 96 percent, over the pre-specified 80 percent futility boundary [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice]. Enrollment stopped at 633 patients, 65 percent fewer than the planned maximum [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice]. After further follow-up the hazard ratio was 2.09 (95% CI 1.38 to 3.17), confirming the superiority of standard chemotherapy [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice]. Berry describes it as, to his knowledge, the first fully Bayesian randomized trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice].

## Pitfalls

The quantity reported at the interim is a posterior probability of failing noninferiority; the design as described in the primary report did not stop on a [[conditional-power]] value [15/calgb-49907-futility-stopping-in-practice].
