## What it is
FDA's meeting types and programs differ in what they can promise, and the differences matter. [sec-regulatory-meetings] The formal types are Type A, B, B (EOP), C, D and INTERACT. [3/formal-meetings-under-pdufa] The programs are the CID and MIDD paired meeting programs, CPIM and DDT qualification. [3/key-points]

## Why it matters
Permissions that matter for Bayesian adaptive designs are all of the form "if FDA agrees", so the agreements are sought in writing, early and on the record. [sec-regulatory-force] A novel design should go to FDA early, with simulations in hand. [3/key-points]

## In the book
- A narrow question with one or two issues goes to Type D. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]
- A stalled program or a safety issue goes to Type A. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]
- A Bayesian, complex adaptive or simulation-driven design goes to the CID program, or Type C otherwise. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]
- A dose that depends on a model goes to the MIDD program. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]
- A general method goes to CPIM, and a biomarker for many sponsors to DDT qualification. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]
- A master protocol goes to Type B pre-IND. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]

## Pitfalls
Minutes are not a binding contract, but a sponsor that departs from documented agreements should expect a question. [3/formal-meetings-under-pdufa]

## Check yourself
- Q: Which meeting fits "Is the phase 3 plan acceptable?" A: Type B (end of phase 2). [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting]
- Q: Which meeting requires no product? A: CPIM. [3/critical-path-innovation-meetings-cpim]
