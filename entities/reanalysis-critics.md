## What it is
Reanalyzing a trial after the result is known has a literature of its own, with supporters and critics. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
An early instance is the debate over ECMO in newborns, in which Ware described the trials and Kass and Greenhouse commented from a Bayesian perspective. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
Wijeysundera and colleagues reanalyzed 88 randomized trials from four general medical journals and concluded that Bayesian and frequentist analyses complement each other. [sec-reanalyses-critics]

## Why it matters
The case for reanalysis is that it states probabilities at clinically meaningful thresholds and that the posterior under several priors makes dependence on the prior visible. [sec-reanalyses-critics]

## In the book
The criticism the book cites is de Grooth and Cremer's commentary that reanalyzing trials using many priors does not contribute to consensus. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
It recommends testing whether a result would persuade a reasonable adversary and assessing bias and generalizability separately, which connects to the skeptical prior standing in for the adversary. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
In the book's own words, a table of many priors invites each reader to pick the row that agrees with prior belief. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
Two further objections follow from the structure: priors are chosen after the result is known, and which trials are reanalyzed is itself an unprespecified choice. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
Brophy's EXCEL reanalysis shows both: adding three other trials found by a stated search lowered the probability of any excess mortality with stenting from 99 to 85 percent. [sec-reanalyses-critics]

## Pitfalls
The book's reading is that each side is right about something different: a reanalysis shows what the data can support, and is weak as evidence when priors and trials were chosen after the fact. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
The rule that follows is to write the reanalysis down before looking, with fixed priors and trials chosen by a stated rule, and to report every prior tried. [sec-reanalyses-critics]

## Check yourself
- Q: What makes the choice of trials in a reanalysis auditable? A: A stated search rule. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
- Q: What does the book treat the result of a reanalysis as? A: Input to the next trial, not a verdict on the last one. [sec-reanalyses-critics]
