## What it is
A final guidance that describes the multiplicity problem and the strategies for handling it when a trial has several endpoints, doses or comparisons. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022] It covers procedures for strong control of the familywise error rate: Bonferroni, Holm, fixed-sequence and gatekeeping procedures, and graphical methods. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022]

## Why it matters
It is the regulatory partner of ICH E9 section 5.6. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022] It sets the expectation that a claim supported by several endpoints comes with a stated multiplicity strategy. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022] The statistical analysis plan states that strategy. [sec-regulatory-traditional]

## In the book
The Bayesian draft echoes the guidance by recommending that the familywise error rate be controlled "across all primary and secondary estimands". [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022] The guidance also leaves room for hierarchical modeling as a complement to, not a replacement for, the confirmatory strategy. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022] Its status is final, October 2022. [sec-regulatory-table]

## Check yourself
- Q: Which procedures does it cover? A: Bonferroni, Holm, fixed-sequence, gatekeeping and graphical methods. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022]
- Q: Can hierarchical modeling replace the confirmatory strategy? A: No, only complement it. [3/multiple-endpoints-in-clinical-trials-final-october-2022]
