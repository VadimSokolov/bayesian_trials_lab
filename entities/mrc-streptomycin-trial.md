## What it is
The 1948 Medical Research Council trial compared streptomycin with bed rest against bed rest alone in acute pulmonary tuberculosis. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed] It is the book's first example of the distinction between sequence generation and concealment. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]

## Why it matters
Hill, who designed the trial, used random allocation, as Fisher had in agricultural experiments. His chief aim was to prevent selection bias in the choice of patients for each arm. He paired it with concealment. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]

## In the book
- What was tested: streptomycin with bed rest against bed rest alone. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]
- Design feature: the allocation was determined from a series of random numbers and kept in sealed envelopes, so the admitting physician decided eligibility before knowing the treatment. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]
- Yoshioka documents how the random numbers and envelopes were actually used. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]

The lesson is that the contribution of the trial was not the coin but the concealment. A list the admitting doctor could see would have recreated the doctor-chosen arm of the confounding simulation. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed] See [[confounding-by-indication]].

The regulatory descendant is 21 CFR 314.126(b)(4), which requires a method of assignment that minimizes bias. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]

## Pitfalls
The book reports the design, not an outcome comparison, for this trial. The point it draws is about process, which is a property of assignment and not of the mathematics of randomness. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed] [sec-randomization-failures]

## Check yourself
- Q: What was the real contribution of the trial, the coin or the concealment? A: The concealment. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]
- Q: Why did the envelopes matter? A: The admitting physician decided eligibility before knowing which treatment the patient would get. [5/the-mrc-streptomycin-trial-1948-concealment-before-the-word-existed]
