## What it is
The Bayesian Analysis Reporting Guidelines (BARG) are one of the few guidelines written for Bayesian analyses, and they are author guidelines. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]
The other Bayesian lists are the seven-item ROBUST checklist and a consensus checklist for clinicians who read a trial analyzed by Bayesian methods. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]

## Why it matters
Generic protocol and reporting checklists have no named place for a prior, a calibrated threshold or a simulation report. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]
BARG helps an author report them. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]

## In the book
A report that gives the prior and its source, the success criterion with its threshold and calibration, the interim schedule and the simulation report, the software and seeds, and the prior sensitivity analyses, each where the generic checklist asks for its counterpart, satisfies the intent of all these lists. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]
The sponsor checklist turns the same content into numbered items. [23/checklists]

## Pitfalls
None of the reporting guidelines around the protocol is a regulatory requirement for a Bayesian trial. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]
The reviewer checklist asks more than a reporting guideline does, for example whether the simulated design is the design that was run. [23/checklists]

## Check yourself
- Q: Is BARG a regulatory requirement? A: No. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]
- Q: Who is the clinicians' consensus checklist for? A: Clinicians who read a trial analyzed by Bayesian methods. [23/structured-protocols-ich-m11-and-spirit-2025]
