## What it is

SHINE (NCT01369069) compared intensive with standard glucose control for 72 hours in acute ischemic stroke with hyperglycemia [16/shine-and-its-bayesian-re-analysis]. Its design combined group sequential stopping with response-adaptive randomization, and it was not a Bayesian trial [16/shine-and-its-bayesian-re-analysis].

## Why it matters

It shows that a confirmatory-scale trial can combine a classical error-controlled stopping rule with limited adaptation of allocation, which is the conservative end of what the regulators describe [16/shine-and-its-bayesian-re-analysis]. The book's practical guidance suggests a group sequential design with futility and efficacy boundaries to protect patients inside a two-arm trial [sec-rar-practice].

## In the book

The trial found no significant difference in favorable 90-day outcome, and severe hypoglycemia occurred in 15 of 581 patients (2.6 percent) on intensive treatment [16/shine-and-its-bayesian-re-analysis]. A later paper compared the Bayesian adaptive design developed in the ADAPT-IT project with the frequentist design that was used, in a retrospective comparison [16/shine-and-its-bayesian-re-analysis].

## Pitfalls

A Bayesian alternative is judged against the classical design by simulation and not by assertion [16/shine-and-its-bayesian-re-analysis].
