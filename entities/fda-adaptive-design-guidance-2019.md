## What it is
The founding FDA document for the book, and it is final. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019] It defines an adaptive design as "a clinical trial design that allows for prospectively planned modifications to one or more aspects of the design based on accumulating data". [sec-regulatory-changes]

## Why it matters
Its four principles are that the chance of erroneous conclusions be adequately controlled, estimation be sufficiently reliable, design details be completely prespecified, and trial integrity be maintained. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019] It says adaptive features can inflate the type I error probability, with repeated looks as the simplest example. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019]

## In the book
- It names simulation for operating characteristics and limited access to comparative interim data. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019]
- It gives an example of effectiveness rules based on a Bayesian posterior probability. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019]
- It works through a single interim look at 50 percent of the information. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019]
- It says simulation is a major use for estimating operating characteristics. [sec-regulatory-changes]

## Pitfalls
The Bayesian draft of 2026 builds on this document, so the 2019 text stays the baseline for both frequentist and Bayesian designs. [3/adaptive-designs-for-clinical-trials-of-drugs-and-biologics-final-november-2019]
