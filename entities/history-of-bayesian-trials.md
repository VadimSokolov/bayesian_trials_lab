## What it is
The decision-theoretic view of trials is as old as the randomized trial. [sec-decision-history] The book gives a short chain of episodes.

## In the book
- Thompson asked in 1933 for the probability that one treatment is better than another and proposed allocating patients accordingly. [sec-decision-history]
- Wald's sequential analysis came from the wartime problem of sampling only as much as needed. [sec-decision-history]
- Grundy, Healy and Rees posed the economic choice of the amount of experimentation in 1956, and Raiffa and Schlaifer's 1961 decision theory supplied the preposterior analysis. [sec-decision-history]
- Anscombe and Colton observed in 1963 that a trial informs the treatment of a much larger population of future patients. [sec-decision-history]
- Novick and Grizzle gave a Bayesian analysis of a four-arm surgical trial in 1965. [sec-decision-history]
- Cornfield gave a Bayesian sequential test in 1966 and set out the Bayesian outlook in 1969. [sec-decision-history]
- Berry restated the case for Bayesian interim analysis in 1985, and Lewis and Berry evaluated Bayesian designs by their classical operating characteristics. [sec-decision-history]
- In January 2026 the FDA issued draft guidance on Bayesian methodology for drugs and biologics, and the EMA released a concept paper for consultation. [sec-decision-history]

## Pitfalls
The objections deserve their strongest form. The prior can decide the answer, the type I error rate of a Bayesian rule is not controlled by the Bayes calculation itself, interim peeking can bias a trial, and a Bayesian design is harder to review. [sec-decision-history] Whitehead's "case for frequentism" is the standard statement of the opposing view. [sec-decision-history]
