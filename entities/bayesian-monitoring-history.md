## What it is

The Bayesian monitoring rule of the chapter has a lineage. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]

## Why it matters

The rule of the chapter keeps the posterior of these earlier proposals and adds the calibration of operating characteristics by simulation that a regulator asks for. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]

## In the book

- Freedman, Lowe and Macaskill proposed stopping rules that incorporate clinical opinion as a prior. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]
- Freedman and Spiegelhalter compared Bayesian monitoring with group sequential methods. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]
- Spiegelhalter, Freedman and Parmar proposed skeptical and enthusiastic reference priors and a range of equivalence, with a rule that stops when the posterior probability of benefit, or of exceeding that range, passes a threshold. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]
- Grossman and colleagues gave a unified method for monitoring and analyzing controlled trials, and Fayers, Ashby and Parmar wrote the tutorial. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]
- Berry argued from the likelihood principle that interim analyses should not be penalized, and Rosner and Berry gave a Bayesian group sequential design for a multiple-arm trial. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]

The wider history of Bayesian trial design is in the book's decision chapter. [sec-ch10-bayeshistory]

## Pitfalls

- Armitage held the other view: the logic of repeated significance tests applies to the trial, and a conclusion must account for how often the data were examined. [11/the-posterior-does-not-depend-on-the-stopping-rule]
