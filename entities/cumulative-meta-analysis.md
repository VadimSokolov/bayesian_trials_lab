## What it is

Jüni and colleagues published a cumulative meta-analysis of the randomized rofecoxib trials, adding one trial at a time in the order of completion. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]

## Why it matters

It shows how evidence built up over time. By the end of 2000 the pool had 52 myocardial infarctions among 20,742 patients and a pooled relative risk of 2.30 (95% CI 1.22 to 4.33, $p = 0.010$). A year later, with 64 events among 21,432 patients, it was 2.24 (1.24 to 4.02, $p = 0.007$). [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]

## In the book

The relative risk showed no heterogeneity by type of control group ($p = 0.41$) or by duration of treatment ($p = 0.82$). [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said] Observational studies put the cardioprotective effect of naproxen at 0.86 (0.75 to 0.99), far too small to explain VIGOR. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said] The authors concluded the drug should have been withdrawn several years earlier. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]

The traditional reading is a significance test on a pooled estimate: by the end of 2000 the interval excluded 1. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]

## Pitfalls

- A p-value of 0.010 does not say how large the harm probably was, how probable a clinically important harm was, or what to do while evidence was accumulating. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]

## Check yourself

- Q: What did the end-2000 interval for the pooled relative risk exclude? A: The value 1. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]
- Q: What does the cumulative p-value leave out? A: The size of the harm and what to do as evidence accumulates. [13/what-the-traditional-analysis-said]
