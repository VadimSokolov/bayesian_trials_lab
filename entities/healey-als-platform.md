## What it is

The Healey ALS Platform Trial is named in the book only as a contrast to EPAD. [20/epad-a-platform-that-never-opened-an-arm] It appears in the chapter on master protocols and platform trials, where platforms are described as machinery that keeps site contracts, database, randomization system, monitoring board and regulatory file standing. [sec-platform]

## Why it matters

The book lists three needs before a platform's first arm: a legal framework that arm sponsors will sign, a governance structure that answers who decides, and a funding model that does not depend on the commercial pipeline the platform is meant to serve. The Healey platform is the contrast on the funding point. [20/epad-a-platform-that-never-opened-an-arm]

## In the book

A platform survives its first arm only if governance is built for arms that are not yet known. Typically a single data monitoring committee oversees all arms, an independent statistical center runs the unblinded interim analyses, and a separate committee approves applications for new arms. [20/governance-firewalls-and-the-monitoring-board] The book's discussion question asks the reader to list what EPAD lacked that Healey ALS had, and which of those a statistician could have fixed. [20/key-points]

## Pitfalls

The book gives no design numbers or results for the Healey platform, so nothing about its operating characteristics should be inferred from this entry. [20/epad-a-platform-that-never-opened-an-arm]

- Q: Which of the three pre-arm needs is organizational rather than statistical? A: All three, as EPAD's lesson is organizational. [20/epad-a-platform-that-never-opened-an-arm]
