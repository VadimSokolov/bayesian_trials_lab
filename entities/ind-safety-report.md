## What it is

Under 21 CFR 312.32 the sponsor must notify FDA and all participating investigators in an IND safety report of a suspected adverse reaction that is both serious and unexpected. The deadline is as soon as possible and no later than 15 calendar days after the sponsor determines the information qualifies. [sec-safety]

An unexpected fatal or life-threatening suspected adverse reaction is due no later than 7 calendar days after the sponsor's initial receipt. [sec-safety]

## Why it matters

The regulation lists the evidence that suggests a causal relationship, among it an aggregate analysis of specific events that occur more frequently in the drug group than in a control group. It is a requirement and prescribes no statistical method for that analysis. [sec-safety]

## In the book

The FDA guidance of December 2025 recommends a safety surveillance plan, aggregate review, and keeping individual treatment assignments blinded to the sponsor's study team, with unblinded aggregate review by a group independent of that team. Guidance recommends and does not require. [sec-safety] ICH E2A supplies the definitions of serious, unexpected and related events. [sec-safety]

The 7-day limit runs from initial receipt for a single interpretable case, whereas the 15-day limit runs from the date the sponsor determines the information qualifies. [sec-safety-reg] For events needing an aggregate analysis, the sponsor makes the determination once the analysis meets the criterion and then reports as soon as possible. [sec-safety-reg]

## Pitfalls

- No monitoring rule shortens or lengthens these limits. [sec-safety-reg]

## Check yourself

- Q: Which clock is shorter, and for what? A: Seven days, for an unexpected fatal or life-threatening reaction, from initial receipt. [sec-safety]
