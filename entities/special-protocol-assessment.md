## What it is
An SPA is a request for FDA's written agreement on the design, endpoints and statistical analysis of certain trials, including trials intended to form the primary basis of an efficacy claim, whatever their phase. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
The current guidance is the April 2018 revision. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]

## Why it matters
If FDA agrees, the agreement is documented and FDA will not change its position on the agreed critical design elements unless a substantial scientific issue essential to determining safety or effectiveness is identified after the trial begins and the sponsor is notified. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
The statute also allows changes by agreement with the sponsor. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
It is worth considering for a Bayesian confirmatory trial because it gets the prior, the criterion and the calibration agreed in writing before the data are seen. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]

## In the book
The timeline lists an SPA request (a written submission with a 45-day review, optionally alongside a Type C meeting) at the phase 3 protocol stage, to agree on the design and analysis. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
A resubmission after FDA comments is reviewed on a 45-day goal. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]

## Pitfalls
FDA will not agree to what it has not seen, so the SAP and the simulation report must be complete. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
The process will expose any design that cannot be defended. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
An SPA does not guarantee approval, or even that FDA will file the application: the agreement is about the design and not the result. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]

## Check yourself
- Q: Does an SPA guarantee approval? A: No, it covers the design and not the result. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
- Q: What can make FDA change an agreed design element? A: A substantial scientific issue essential to safety or effectiveness found after the trial begins, or agreement with the sponsor. [23/the-interaction-timeline-with-fda]
