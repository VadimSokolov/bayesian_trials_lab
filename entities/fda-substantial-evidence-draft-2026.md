## What it is
*Demonstrating Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness for Human Drug and Biological Products* is a revised draft guidance of June 2026. [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft] It revises the draft issued in December 2019, and as a draft it is not for implementation. [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft] The revision was announced in the Federal Register on 24 June 2026 under docket FDA-2019-D-4964. [3/the-1997-amendment-one-trial-and-confirmatory-evidence] When finalized it will replace the 1998 guidance. [3/the-1997-amendment-one-trial-and-confirmatory-evidence]

## Why it matters
It describes the conventional two-trial route and a single-trial route, which "will involve a highly persuasive trial or a source of strong confirmatory evidence". [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft] It lists the factors FDA weighs: the seriousness of the disease and unmet need, the strength of the single trial, the strength and source of the confirmatory evidence, and whether it is ethical and practicable to conduct more than one trial. [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft]

## In the book
- Confirmatory evidence may be of types that are not trials, such as mechanistic evidence. [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft]
- The plan for one trial should be discussed with FDA before it starts. [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft]
- The draft explicitly recognizes Bayesian analyses. [3/the-2026-substantial-evidence-draft]

## Pitfalls
Check the date on the document itself: the address of the 2019 draft now serves the June 2026 revision. [sec-regulatory-reading] A draft is nonbinding and subject to change. [sec-regulatory-force]
