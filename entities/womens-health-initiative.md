## What it is

The estrogen plus progestin trial of the Women's Health Initiative, in healthy postmenopausal women, was stopped early for harm on the recommendation of its data monitoring committee. The principal results were published in JAMA in July 2002. [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]

## Why it matters

It shows a stop for harm as a decision under uncertainty, made with discretion by people who see unblinded data and are independent of the sponsor. [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]

## In the book

At the board's recommendation on 31 May 2002, after a mean of 5.2 years of follow-up, the test statistic for invasive breast cancer had exceeded the stopping boundary for that adverse effect and the global index statistic supported risks exceeding benefits. [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]

Hazard ratios with nominal 95% confidence intervals: 1.26 (1.00 to 1.59) for invasive breast cancer (290 cases), 1.29 (1.02 to 1.63) for coronary heart disease (286 cases), 1.41 (1.07 to 1.85) for stroke, and 1.15 (1.03 to 1.28) for the global index. Total mortality was not affected, at 0.98 (0.82 to 1.18). [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]

## Pitfalls

- The nominal interval for breast cancer reaches 1.00. The stop rested on the crossing of a sequential boundary, not on that interval. [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]
- Safety monitoring in such a trial is not a formal alpha-controlled test of a single endpoint. The committee weighs a pattern of outcomes. [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]

- Q: Was total mortality affected? A: No, the hazard ratio was 0.98. [11/a-stop-for-harm-the-womens-health-initiative-2002]
