## What it is
The force of a document varies, so the book sorts sources into categories. [sec-regulatory-force] A statute binds, a regulation binds, and a guidance "contains nonbinding recommendations". [sec-regulatory]

## Why it matters
A sponsor should never describe a draft as "FDA requirements" in a protocol or meeting package. [sec-regulatory-force] A permission is not an entitlement. [sec-regulatory-force] The permissions that matter most to Bayesian adaptive designs are all of the form "if FDA agrees". [sec-regulatory-force]

## In the book
- Requires: FD&C Act 505(d), the FDAMA 1997 single-trial provision, FDORA 2022, and 21 CFR 314.126. [sec-regulatory-force]
- Recommends: final guidances such as Adaptive Designs 2019, draft guidances such as Bayesian Methodology (January 2026), and ICH Step 2 and Step 4 documents. [sec-regulatory-force]
- Permits: one trial plus confirmatory evidence, non-calibrated Bayesian success criteria with FDA agreement, and nonconcurrent controls in rare circumstances. [sec-regulatory-force]
- Process: formal meetings and the CID and MIDD programs define how to ask, not the evidentiary standard. [sec-regulatory-force]

## Pitfalls
A guidance is nonbinding, but a departure without prior discussion is a risk to the program. [sec-regulatory-fda]

## Check yourself
- Q: "FDA requires a simulation report for any Bayesian design." Accurate? A: No, the Bayesian draft recommends it and is only a draft. [3/key-points]
- Q: Is a permission an entitlement? A: No, it is available only if the agency concurs. [sec-regulatory-force]
