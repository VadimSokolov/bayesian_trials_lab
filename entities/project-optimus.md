## What it is
The final guidance of August 2024 (docket FDA-2022-D-2827, Oncology Center of Excellence with CDER and CBER) was developed through Project Optimus, launched in 2021. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks] It recommends that oncology sponsors compare multiple dosages, using pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, safety and efficacy information, rather than default to the MTD. It is a guidance: it states current thinking and does not bind. [sec-dose]

## Why it matters
In paraphrase, it asks that doses taken to registrational trials be chosen on more than the MTD, including late and low-grade toxicities. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks] Dose-ranging should compare several dosages, where feasible with randomization, to characterize dose-response and dose-toxicity, not to prove superiority. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks] Model-based and Bayesian methods are expected to be useful, and the guidance neither requires a specific design nor excludes the 3+3. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks]

## Pitfalls
The guidance does not address first-in-human starting-dose selection. It does not specifically address radiopharmaceuticals, cellular and gene therapies, oncolytic viruses, microbiota, cancer vaccines or pediatric development. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks] ICH E4 had already set these principles in 1994. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks] Exposure-response models fall under ICH M15. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks]

## Check yourself
- Q: Does the guidance require a model-based design? A: No, and it does not exclude the 3+3. [17/what-the-fda-guidance-asks]
- Q: What would a reviewer ask about a dose that does not maximize efficacy? A: The probability of choosing a dose that is too low or too toxic under each scenario. [17/randomized-dose-comparison-and-what-a-regulator-will-ask]
