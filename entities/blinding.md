## What it is
Randomization protects the comparison against bias in who receives each treatment. Blinding, also called masking, protects it against bias in what happens afterward: how patients report symptoms, how investigators care for them and how outcomes are judged. [sec-randomization-blinding]

ICH E9 defines a double-blind trial as one in which neither the subject nor any of the investigator or sponsor staff involved in treatment or evaluation are aware of the treatment received. [sec-randomization-blinding]

## Why it matters
The book lists open label, single blind, double blind, triple blind (also the data analysts) and blinded outcome assessment. Blinding matters most when outcomes are subjective, such as pain, depression or symptom scores. [sec-randomization-blinding]

## In the book
RECOVERY compared dexamethasone with usual care without blinding. The primary outcome was death within 28 days, an objective endpoint that limits assessment bias. [sec-randomization-blinding] When masking is impossible, as with surgery or devices, the protocol should use blinded outcome assessment and objective endpoints. [sec-randomization-blinding]

Maintaining the blind takes work: matching placebos, a double-dummy design when comparators differ in form, independent unblinded staff and a prespecified emergency unblinding procedure. [sec-randomization-blinding]

## Pitfalls
Side effects can unblind, such as nausea, a rash or a visible lab change. [sec-randomization-blinding] The DMC and its statistician see unblinded data and the sponsor does not, so the charter should state who sees what and when. [sec-randomization-blinding]

## Check yourself
- Q: When is an open-label trial still informative? A: When the outcome is objective, as with death within 28 days in RECOVERY. [sec-randomization-blinding]
- Q: Can a trial be properly blinded without concealment? A: No. An investigator who knows the next assignment already knows the arm. [sec-randomization-concealment]
