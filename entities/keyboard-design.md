## What it is
The Keyboard design of Yan and colleagues lays equal-width keys, each as wide as the target interval, over the DLT-rate axis. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard] It recommends the move toward the key with the highest posterior probability. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard]

## Why it matters
Like BOIN and mTPI, its decisions are printed as a table by number of DLTs and number treated, so the logic can be checked by hand. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard]

## In the book
The book mentions Keyboard alongside mTPI and mTPI-2 and does not simulate it. Which design to choose is an empirical question, and the evidence is simulation. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard] Published comparisons looked at accuracy, safety and reliability of several newer phase 1 designs. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard]

## Pitfalls
Those comparisons are in stated scenarios at stated sample sizes. The protocol should report the chosen design's own operating characteristics in scenarios that include a badly placed target. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard]

## Check yourself
- Q: How wide is each key? A: As wide as the target interval. [17/the-other-interval-designs-mtpi-mtpi-2-and-keyboard]
