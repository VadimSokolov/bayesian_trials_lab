## What it is

Three independently funded Bayesian platforms (REMAP-CAP, ATTACC, an international platform led from Canada, and ACTIV-4a in the United States) asked the same question early in the pandemic: therapeutic-dose against prophylactic-dose anticoagulation in COVID-19. [20/the-covid-anticoagulation-multiplatform]

## Why it matters

Separate reports would have invited conflicting results and a post hoc meta-analysis. The teams harmonized protocols and statistical analysis plans before the data were in, and combined patient-level data in a hierarchical model with dynamic borrowing across severity strata. [20/the-covid-anticoagulation-multiplatform] The shared object was a pre-agreed analysis, not a shared control group. [20/the-covid-anticoagulation-multiplatform]

## In the book

The stopping rule was superiority at posterior probability above 99% or futility at above 95% for an odds ratio below 1.2, applied separately by severity cohort. In critically ill patients enrollment stopped for futility (adjusted odds ratio 0.83, 95% CrI 0.67 to 1.03, futility probability 99.9%). In non-critically ill patients therapeutic dosing increased organ-support-free days (odds ratio 1.27, 95% CrI 1.03 to 1.58, probability of benefit 98.6%). [20/the-covid-anticoagulation-multiplatform]

## Pitfalls

The finding was not a post hoc subgroup because the severity strata were specified in advance. [20/the-covid-anticoagulation-multiplatform] The approach works only when sponsors agree on the estimand beforehand. [20/the-covid-anticoagulation-multiplatform]
