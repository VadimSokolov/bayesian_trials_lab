## What it is
DDT qualification is a formal process by which FDA qualifies a tool, such as a biomarker, a clinical outcome assessment or an animal model, for a stated context of use. [3/drug-development-tool-ddt-qualification] Once qualified, any sponsor can use the tool within that context without FDA re-reviewing its suitability in each application. [3/drug-development-tool-ddt-qualification]

## Why it matters
The context of use is the key idea: a tool is qualified for a purpose, not in the abstract. [3/drug-development-tool-ddt-qualification] For trial design the relevance is endpoints, since a qualified tool is the kind of "well-defined and reliable method of assessing response" that 21 CFR 314.126 asks for. [3/drug-development-tool-ddt-qualification] The 21st Century Cures Act put the process on a statutory footing. [3/drug-development-tool-ddt-qualification]

## In the book
The meeting chooser lists DDT qualification for a biomarker or outcome assessment that many sponsors will use. [3/how-to-choose-the-meeting] It is slow and typically pursued by consortia. [3/drug-development-tool-ddt-qualification]
