## Summary
This chapter explains the legal and regulatory frame in which a trial is designed. It begins with the statutory standard of substantial evidence in section 505(d) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, the 1997 amendment that allows one trial with confirmatory evidence, the regulation 21 CFR 314.126 on what adequate and well controlled means, and the 2026 draft on substantial evidence. It describes what a traditional submission looks like and what changes with a Bayesian adaptive design. It then goes through the ICH principles (E3, E6(R3), E8(R1), E9, E9(R1), E11A, E20, M4, M15), the FDA design guidances (adaptive designs, master protocols, multiple endpoints, optimizing dosage, external controls, AI credibility), the history of Bayesian methods at the regulators before 2026 and the January 2026 FDA draft on Bayesian methodology with the response to it. It separates what the documents require, recommend and permit, describes how to interact with FDA (PDUFA meetings, the Complex Innovative Trial Design and MIDD paired meeting programs, CPIM, drug development tool qualification), and gives the EMA and MHRA counterparts. Case studies include REBYOTA, BNT162b2, pediatric belimumab, and the devices Superion and OPTIMIZER Smart.
