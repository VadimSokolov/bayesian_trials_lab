## Summary
This chapter asks why trials randomize and what randomization buys. It sets out the counterfactual problem and confounding by indication, what goes wrong without randomization, and the methods: simple, permuted block, stratified randomization and minimization, with how a schedule is actually generated and why allocation must be concealed. It develops randomization inference, discusses what randomness is (incompressibility and Kolmogorov complexity, and Arnold's measure), blinding, and intention to treat with the estimands framework and intercurrent events. It then asks why a Bayesian still randomizes, through ignorability of the assignment mechanism and the Bayesian arguments for randomizing. A long section covers covariate adjustment: ANCOVA, change from baseline, analyzing as you randomized, not testing baseline balance, binary and time to event outcomes, estimators robust to model misspecification, what FDA and EMA say, and the Bayesian version. It ends with what the protocol should state. Case studies are the MRC streptomycin trial, RECOVERY and the Women's Health Initiative.
