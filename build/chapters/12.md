## Summary
This chapter asks what validity at every stopping time requires, and what it costs. It begins with what a group sequential design does not cover (unplanned looks and continuous monitoring), then defines e-values as bets, test martingales and Ville's inequality, a betting martingale for a binary endpoint, the growth rate (how big a bet), and the odds of Cornfield in 1966. It covers likelihood ratio, mixture and process e-values (sequences of e-values indexed by time), passes from e-values to p-values and confidence sequences, and states the surcharge that validity at every stopping time costs. It relates e-values to Bayes factors, compares the power and stopping time of e-value, Bayesian and group sequential designs on a two arm binary design and a time to event design reconstructed from SELECT, and explains why the orderings differ. It adds a Bayes risk comparison, asks when a sponsor should pay the surcharge, shows a negative result for the mixture e-value with many subgroups at once, and gives what the regulator will ask. The case is BNT162b2, which had several interim looks on a schedule fixed in the statistical analysis plan.
