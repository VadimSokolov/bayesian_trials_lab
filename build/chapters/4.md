## Summary
This chapter builds the comparison of two treatments from the ground up with simulated and real trial data. It starts with one trial and two endpoints, reruns it many times, and derives the randomization test, the normal approximation and the z test. It defines the p-value carefully, explains confidence intervals and their duality with tests, and defines type I error, type II error and power. It explains why a one sided level of 0.025 is used, and covers noninferiority and equivalence against a margin, with the SURPASS CVOT noninferiority margin as an example. It closes with where the traditional trial stands and what changes next. Examples are RECOVERY (a rate ratio and an interval) and ANDROMEDA SHOCK (what not significant does and does not mean). The chapter proves nothing new; it sets down the traditional machinery that later chapters compare against.
