MAJOR docs/03-errors-power.html:485 | "Type I: approving a useless drug" | A Type I error is a trial concluding efficacy when the drug has none; approval is a separate regulatory decision that weighs the whole dossier (often two trials, safety, benefit-risk). MAJOR docs/00-are-they-different.html:177 | "true response rate (the share of all patients it would help)" | A response rate is the share who respond while on the drug, not the share it helps; some would have responded on placebo. The page's own placebo also has a response rate, which under this wording would be "the share placebo helps". This page is about potential outcomes, so the slip teaches the exact confusion the page then untangles. | "The drug's true response rate (the share of all patients who would improve on it) is hidden; in half the trials it equals the placebo's."

MINOR docs/00-are-they-different.html:216 | "When patients choose, the less sick ones tend to pick the drug." | Direction of selection varies: healthy-user bias goes this way, but confounding by indication (linked from this page) usually goes the other way, with sicker patients more likely to be treated, making a drug look worse.
