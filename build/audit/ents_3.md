# Audit: encyclopedia entities, batch 3 (78 pages)

MAJOR time-trend-bias:What it is | "receives more of the later, healthier patients" | Drift in the background response rate (better standard of care, changing site mix) does not make later patients healthy; trial patients all have the condition. The point is that later patients respond better whatever arm they are on. The same wording is in the book, 16/the-mechanism (BOOK?). | "An arm that benefited from early luck receives more of the later patients, who respond better on any treatment, and looks better still."
MAJOR rebyota-trial:In the book | "one-sided type I errors of 0.025 (one trial) and 0.00125 (two trials)" | Two trials at one-sided 0.025 give 0.025^2 = 0.000625 one-sided (substantial-evidence page: 1/1600). 0.00125 is the two-sided form (0.05^2/2), and the adjusted threshold 0.9993275 (1 - c = 0.00067) sits next to 0.000625, not 0.00125. So the pages contradict each other. Medium confidence. The book says "one-sided 0.00125" in 3/what-the-standard-implies-for-the-numbers, 14/rebyota-two-thresholds..., sec-borrowing-rebyota (BOOK?): check the CBER briefing document. | "analogues of a one-sided type I error of 0.025 (one trial) and of 0.00125 two-sided, i.e. 0.000625 one-sided (two trials)" (or whatever the briefing document states)

MINOR time-trend-bias:Pitfalls | "Plain Thompson stays near 0.05 even when stratified" | The page never says the nominal level is one-sided 0.025, so 0.05 can read as acceptable. It is twice nominal (book 16/key-points: "against 0.025 nominal"). | "Even with stratification, plain Thompson keeps a type I error near 0.05, twice the nominal one-sided 0.025, until the critical value is calibrated by simulation."
MINOR equivalence-trial:What it is | "This is the same as the confidence interval lying entirely inside" | No level is given. TOST at level alpha per side matches a (1 - 2 alpha) interval: 90% for bioequivalence at 0.05, 95% when each side is tested at 0.025. A reader may assume a 95% interval always. | "This is the same as the (1 - 2 alpha) confidence interval lying entirely inside (-log M, log M): 90% for the usual bioequivalence test at 0.05 per side, 95% at 0.025 per side."
MINOR lindleys-paradox:Why it matters | "This is why very large trials sometimes find significant effects that are clinically trivial" | This mixes up Lindley's paradox (a fixed p-value is weaker evidence as n grows) with high power detecting tiny effects. That second point is true without any Bayesian argument. | "A related point is that very large trials can reach significance for clinically trivial effects. Lindley's paradox adds that the same p-value in a larger trial is weaker evidence against the null."
MINOR aggregate-safety-assessment:summary and What it is | "A sponsor's safety review ... The unblinded DMC review compares arms" | The DMC is independent, and the sponsor does not see its comparative results. Listing it as a layer of the sponsor's review blurs the DMC/sponsor firewall that the brief asks to keep clear. | "A trial's safety review is not one statistic. Four layers sit side by side; the sponsor runs three, and the unblinded comparison of arms belongs to the independent DMC, whose comparative results the sponsor does not see."
MINOR blinded-pooled-review:summary | "exceeds what comparable untreated patients would show" | Participants usually get standard of care (often add-on designs), so the reference is the expected background rate in similar patients with the condition who are not on the investigational drug, not untreated people. | "...to see whether it exceeds the background rate expected in comparable patients not receiving the investigational drug."
