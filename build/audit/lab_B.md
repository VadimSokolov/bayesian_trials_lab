# Audit lab_B: modules 04 to 08 (clinical and statistical realism)

MAJOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:71 | "Randomization made the two groups alike." | Teaches that the coin guarantees balance; randomization balances known and unknown factors only on average (in expectation), and any one trial can be unbalanced by chance (the page's own icon panel shows this). Same idea repeated at line 329 ("Counting everyone keeps the groups alike"). | "Randomization made the two groups alike on average. What can still go wrong?" and at 329: "Counting everyone keeps the coin's balance, so on average a useless drug shows about 0."
MINOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:166 | "Dropping quitters makes the groups unequal again." | Contradicts the page's own card at 341-345, where random quitting leaves the groups alike and per protocol is unbiased; the bias needs quitting linked to prognosis. | "Dropping quitters can make the groups unequal again, when the patients who quit differ (for example, are sicker) from those who stay."
MINOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:87 | "Patients who quit get no help from the drug." | Stated as a fact about trials; it is a simulation assumption (in reality partial exposure can help or harm, and quitters may switch to other treatment). | "In this simulation, patients who quit get no help from the drug."
MINOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:358 | "A look-alike dummy pill (placebo) removes that." | A placebo does not remove expectation effects; it gives both groups the same expectations so the effect cancels out of the comparison. | "A look-alike dummy pill (placebo) gives both groups the same hopes, so they cancel out of the comparison."
MINOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:163 | "Hide who got what with a look-alike placebo" | Presented as the universal remedy; a placebo is not always ethical or possible (add-on to standard of care, active comparator with double dummy, surgery), and blinded outcome assessment is the fallback when patients cannot be blinded (as in RECOVERY, cited on the same page). | "Hide who got what where possible (a look-alike placebo or double dummy); when patients cannot be blinded, keep the outcome assessors blind and prefer objective endpoints."
MINOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:396 (blinding levels) | "Triple blind: Patients, doctors and the analyst do not know." | Omits outcome assessors, often the most important group to blind; "single/double/triple blind" are used inconsistently, which is why CONSORT asks trials to name who was blinded. | Add after the levels: "These labels are used inconsistently; good reports say exactly who was blinded: patients, clinicians, outcome assessors, analysts."
MINOR docs/06-fair-comparisons.html:384 | "17% fewer deaths overall, 36% fewer on ventilators (rate ratios 0.83 and 0.64)" | Relative reduction given without saying so; readers may read it as absolute (28-day mortality was 22.9% vs 25.7%, about 3 points absolute). | "...: 17% fewer deaths overall in relative terms (22.9% vs 25.7% died by day 28), 36% fewer among patients on ventilators (rate ratios 0.83 and 0.64)."
MINOR docs/04-multiplicity.html:99 | "The p-value asks how often a useless drug would look this good." | Missing "at least"; the p-value is the chance of a result at least this extreme if the drug is useless. | "The p-value asks how often a useless drug would look at least this good by luck alone."
MINOR docs/04-multiplicity.html:179 and 444 | "A drug helps 30% of patients at every site." | Equates a 30% response rate on the drug with the drug helping 30%; some patients improve without treatment (the page itself contrasts drug with placebo elsewhere). | "30% of patients on the drug improve at every site."
