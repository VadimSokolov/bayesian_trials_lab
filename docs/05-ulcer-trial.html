Pulling extreme results toward the middle (shrinkage)

The best and worst arms are partly luck. Shrinkage pulls each arm toward the overall average, and pulls harder when the arm has fewer patients. Same counts as above (illustrative); edit the table to see it move.

A hierarchical model does this pulling in a principled way. Module 9 shows the same idea as borrowing from history.

Regulator's corner

Why decide the comparison before looking? Put the main analysis choices in the protocol before the trial. Finish the detailed plan before unblinding (ICH E9, section 5.1).

ICH E9 Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials (PDF)

Takeaway

  • A p-value just above 0.05 does not mean the treatments are equal.
  • Each test answers one question. Ask which one, before you look.
  • Shrinkage pulls extreme arms toward the average, most for small arms, so picking the best and worst fools you less.
  • Pick best vs worst after the fact, and chance fools you about 1 time in 20 (5.2% here), over ten times more than with a pair chosen in advance (0.4%).
