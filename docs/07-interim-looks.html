Will the trial win if it keeps going?

Posterior: how sure we are today that the drug is better. Predictive probability: the chance the trial ends in a win if it runs to the end, given the data so far.

Real trials that stopped early

  • CALGB 49907 (cancer trial): stopped early.
  • ASTIN (stroke drug): stopped for futility. The trial had little chance of meeting its planned goal, so enrollment stopped.
  • DAWN (clot removal after stroke): stopped enrollment at 206 patients because the predictive probability of a win (module 7, below) passed 95%. At the end, the chance clot removal was better was above 99.9%.

Sources: Sokolova and Sokolov (2026), Clinical Trials in Practice, chapter 12, which cites Krams et al. (2003, ASTIN) and Nogueira et al. (2018), NEJM, DAWN.

Regulator's corner

Trial rules (ICH E9): plan every look in advance and write it in the protocol (the trial plan).

"any analysis intended to compare treatment arms with respect to efficacy or safety at any time prior to the formal completion of a trial"

In plain words: any look at whether the drug is working before the trial ends.

ICH E9, section 4.5. Official PDF.

Takeaway

  • Every extra look is another chance for a false alarm.
  • Plan the looks first, and use stricter bars so false alarms stay at 2.5%.
  • Stopping early for benefit saves patients, but the estimate from a trial that stops early tends to be too big.
  • Predictive probability is the chance of a win at the end, not how sure we are today.
